# The Engine Model: Value, Growth, Fulfillment

> The lens your AIOS organizes around. Engine framing popularized by Ryan Deiss (Get Scalable); the AIOS application is Helion's.

You are not running a business. You are building a machine that runs the business. Helion calls that machine your **Value Engine**.

A Value Engine takes in raw inputs (attention, leads, time, raw work) and turns out value (customers, revenue, delivered promises) without you touching every step. Your AIOS is the Value Engine for your own work. The whole point is that it compounds. Each week it knows more, reaches more, does more, and runs more on its own.

A Value Engine runs on two smaller engines. Every connection you wire and every skill you build belongs to one of them.

## The Growth Engine: acquire and expand revenue

Everything that brings money in or makes existing customers worth more.

- Find and attract the right people (content, lead gen, outreach, ads)
- Convert them (follow up, nurture, sales)
- Expand them (retention, upsell, win-back, referrals)

If a skill helps you get a customer or grow what a customer is worth, it lives in the Growth Engine.

## The Fulfillment Engine: deliver the promise and keep it

Everything that delivers what you sold and keeps customers happy enough to stay.

- Onboard and set up (intake, kickoff, account setup)
- Do the work (delivery, production, the actual service)
- Support and report (questions, fixes, status, quality checks)

If a skill helps you deliver, support, or run operations without dropping quality, it lives in the Fulfillment Engine.

## Why this is the lens

Most people automate at random. They build whatever feels clever that week. Six months later they have a pile of disconnected automations and no idea if any of it moved the business.

The Engine Model fixes that with one question you ask of every build:

> **"Which engine does this serve, Growth or Fulfillment?"**

If you cannot answer, you are automating noise. Kill it or rescope it.

Your AIOS is "complete" when both engines are covered across all four build layers (see `the-architecture.md`). That is what `/audit` scores. That is what `/level-up` fills in, one piece at a time.

## How it ties to where value actually comes from

Every automation worth building moves one of three numbers. Two belong to Growth, one to Fulfillment.

| Number | Engine |
|---|---|
| More customers | Growth |
| More value per customer | Growth |
| Lower cost to deliver | Fulfillment |

If a build does not move one of those three, you do not build it. "Because it is cool" is not a reason.
